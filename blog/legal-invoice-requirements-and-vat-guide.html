Legal Invoice Requirements & VAT Rules Explained

Published on 29 August 2026 • 5 min read

An invoice is a legally binding demand for payment. If essential statutory information is omitted, clients and corporate accounts departments have valid grounds to reject the document, delaying your payment.

Mandatory Elements for All Standard Invoices

  • Unique Sequential Identifier: A distinct reference number with no random gaps (e.g., INV-2026-001).
  • Your Full Business Details: Legal trading name, registered address, and contact information.
  • Client Information: The full name or trading entity of your customer, along with their billing address.
  • Dates: The official Date of Issue and explicit Due Date.
  • Itemized Descriptions: Clear breakdown of goods or services provided, including quantity, unit price, and subtotal per line.
  • Total Amount Payable: Clear currency denomination (GBP, USD, EUR) and payment instructions.

Additional Rules for VAT-Registered Businesses

If your business is VAT-registered, your document must be formatted as a valid VAT Invoice:

  • VAT Registration Number: Display your 9-digit VAT number prominently (e.g., GB 123 4567 89).
  • Tax Point (Time of Supply): The supply date if different from the invoice issue date.
  • Per-Item Net Prices: The cost per unit excluding VAT.
  • Applicable VAT Rates: The specific rate charged per line item (e.g., Standard 20%, Reduced 5%, or Zero 0%).
  • Tax Breakdown: A separate line displaying the total net amount, total VAT charged, and final gross total.

Statutory Record-Keeping

Tax authorities require businesses to retain copies of all issued sales invoices and purchase receipts for a minimum of 5 to 6 years for auditing purposes.
